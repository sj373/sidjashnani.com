Why most sales training fades, and what to do instead

If you run a business, there's a good chance you've paid for sales training at some point, watched your team enjoy the day, and then noticed a month later that everyone was back to waiting for the phone to ring. That's a very common experience, and it usually has nothing to do with the quality of the trainer or the effort of your people. This page explains why it happens, using Alex Goldfayn's thinking in Outgrow, and what Sid Jashnani, CEO of Outgrow and Outgrow Certified Advisor, does differently with the teams he works with.

Why sales training so often doesn't stick

In Outgrow, Alex lists the reasons most sales organisations stay reactive, and several of them explain why training on its own rarely changes much. The first is that most growth initiatives focus on mechanics instead of mindset. A course can teach a salesperson what to say, but if they're privately worried about bothering the customer or being turned down, they'll find reasons not to make the call. Alex puts it plainly: behaviour follows thinking, and it's impossible for salespeople to outsell their mindset.

The second reason is history. Experienced salespeople have usually seen several growth initiatives come and go, and Alex describes how they tend to treat each new one as a flavour of the month. They nod along, wait it out, and things drift back to the reactive default.

The third is accountability. Salespeople are often independent and busy, and many leaders aren't sure how to hold them to new behaviours without it feeling like policing. When nobody looks at whether the new habit is happening, it quietly stops.

Doing the work every day matters more than knowing about it

Alex describes Outgrow as a doing programme rather than a training programme, and Outgrow's own website puts it as "sales doing, not sales training". What that means in practice is that most salespeople already know they ought to call customers, follow up on quotes and ask for referrals. What's usually missing is a simple system that gets them to do those things every day, together, and that makes the effort visible each week. When knowledge isn't backed by daily practice it fades quickly, whereas a small habit that's tracked and talked about every week tends to keep going.

That's why the Outgrow approach to sales training is to keep the learning short and spend most of the effort on the doing. Your people learn a handful of proactive communications, practise them on real customers, and then make a few of them every single day. In Outgrow, Alex shows that each one takes seconds or a few minutes, whether it's a "did you know" question about another product, a quote follow-up, a referral request or a pivot to the sale. Because the time required is so small, nobody has to give up their day job to do it.

The manager is where the habit lives

Sid has written in PREDICTABLE that when you train the frontline you get a good week, and when you train the manager you get a good year. A salesperson can make a great start, but it's the manager who keeps the habit going across the whole team, week after week. In an Outgrow company, the manager runs a short weekly huddle where the team reviews its scorecard, shares real wins, and agrees which customers to contact and what to talk to them about. Sid's advice is to always connect the activity back to revenue, because a meeting that only asks "did you make your calls?" soon feels like surveillance, while one that shows what the calls opened up keeps everyone interested.

Measuring the effort that leads to sales

Outgrow tracks swings, not hits. In other words, your team is measured on the proactive efforts they make, which they can control, rather than on whether each conversation turns into an order, which depends on the customer. This takes pressure off your people and gives you leading indicators of where sales are heading, rather than only finding out at the end of the quarter.

What this looks like for business owners in the UK

For a business owner, the practical difference is that instead of booking a one-off event, you put a small daily routine in place and give your managers a simple way to look after it. The aim, for teams who keep it up, is a target of 15–30% organic sales growth a year.

FAQs

Why doesn't traditional sales training stick?

In Outgrow, Alex Goldfayn points to three common causes: training focuses on mechanics instead of mindset, salespeople have seen previous initiatives fade, and most companies have no simple way to keep people accountable once the training day is over.

What does "sales doing, not sales training" mean?

It's how Outgrow describes its approach. Your team already knows much of what to do, so the emphasis goes on doing a few proactive communications every day and looking at those efforts together each week, rather than on learning new theory.

How much time does proactive selling take each day?

Alex designed the Outgrow communications to take seconds or a few minutes each, so a handful of them fits into a normal day.

Is this suitable for business owners rather than large sales departments?

Yes. Sid works with leadership teams of mid-market businesses, and in Outgrow the owner or CEO sets the tone while managers run the weekly rhythm. Everyone who talks to customers can take part, not only people with "sales" in their job title.

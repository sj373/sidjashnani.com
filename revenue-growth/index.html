Revenue growth from the customers you already have

When a business wants to grow, the conversation usually turns to new markets, new products, new hires or more leads. Those all have their place, but there's a quieter source of growth that most companies overlook, and it's sitting with the customers who already buy from you and already trust you. This page explains how Sid Jashnani, CEO of Outgrow and Outgrow Certified Advisor, helps mid-market leadership teams grow revenue organically and intentionally from that existing base, using the Outgrow method created by Alex Goldfayn.

What organic, intentional growth means

In Outgrow, Alex tells the story of a CEO who said, "I have a revenue problem." His company had grown, but on closer inspection the growth came from price rises and acquisitions, and his salespeople had created very little new volume themselves. Organic growth is the part that comes from your own people selling more to customers and prospects, and it's the kind Outgrow focuses on.

The word intentional matters too. Alex describes Outgrow as an easy system of intentional relationship building. Rather than hoping customers remember to ask you for more, your team decides who to get in touch with each week, makes those calls, and keeps a note of what they find out. Growth that's planned this way is much easier to forecast than growth that depends on whoever happens to ring in.

Where the growth is hiding in your customer base

Alex's experience across many business-to-business companies is that most sales teams spend the great majority of their time with a small group of customers, roughly a fifth of them, who call often and with whom relationships are already good. The other customers get far less attention. Some are on autopilot, buying a few things from you and the rest from competitors. Some order online or by email without ever speaking to anyone. Some are slowly buying less, and some have stopped altogether without anybody noticing. Alex calls these quieter customers your lowest-hanging fruit for sales growth, because you've already done the hard work of winning them.

Most customers only know part of what you sell

Alex also points out that most customers have you niched. They buy one category from you and other things from someone else, often simply because it never occurred to them that you offer those things too, and because nobody on your side thought to mention it. Sid has written in PREDICTABLE about how, while scaling his own business to £27M+ revenue, he never once asked a customer what share of their spend came to him. He now suggests asking it on an ordinary call, "Roughly what share of your spend in this category comes our way at the moment?", and then following up with "What would have to be true for that to be a bigger number?"

The simple communications that bring it in

Outgrow gives your team a few short communications to use with these customers. There's the proactive call to someone you haven't spoken to for a while, the "did you know" question that mentions another product or service, and the "what else" question that invites the customer to tell you what else they need. There's the follow-up on quotes that would otherwise go quiet, and the request for a referral from a happy customer. Finally there are the two pivots: to the sale, when you ask for the business, and to the next conversation, when you agree when you'll speak again. Each of these takes seconds or a few minutes, which is why a busy team can do several every day.

A practical first step: list your customers

A useful place to start is with a few lists. In one PREDICTABLE essay, Sid describes sitting with a business owner and splitting his customers into top, middle and smallest accounts, then adding prospects he'd quoted and lost, and customers who had stopped buying. Asking one simple question about each group showed him how much business was already within reach, from people who knew and trusted him. In Outgrow, Alex recommends building similar customer lists so that your people never have to wonder who to call.

Making the growth predictable

The reason this kind of growth can be forecast is that it's built on behaviours your team controls. Outgrow tracks swings, not hits, so each week you can see how many proactive efforts were made and what they led to. Alex treats those efforts as leading indicators, which means they show you where sales are heading before the numbers arrive. With the habit running consistently, the target is 15–30% organic revenue growth a year, tracked week by week.

FAQs

Why focus on existing customers rather than new leads?

Your existing customers already know and trust you, and most of them buy only part of what you offer. Alex Goldfayn calls the customers your team rarely speaks to the lowest-hanging fruit for sales growth, because the hard work of winning them has already been done.

What does organic revenue growth mean?

It's growth that comes from your own people selling more, as opposed to growth from price increases or acquisitions. That's the growth Outgrow is built to create.

Does this replace looking for new customers?

No. Outgrow is about communicating proactively with customers and prospects, so new business is part of it too. The emphasis is on making the most of the relationships you already have, which is usually where the quickest gains are.

How quickly could we see results?

That depends on your business and how consistently your team makes its proactive efforts. Outgrow measures those efforts every week so you can see the leading indicators early, and the target the work is aimed at is 15–30% organic revenue growth a year.

A CEO sat across from me last month and said: “I’ve got a sales team.

A CEO sat across from me last month and said: *“I’ve got a sales team. A CRM. A methodology my last consultant left behind. And a coach who shows up every quarter. So why does my revenue still wobble?”*

I told him what was missing.

He stared at me. Then he said: *“Why does no one talk about this?”*

That’s the post.

---

**The stack everyone has. The part nobody can name.**

Walk into any £5M+ business and you’ll see the same stack.

A sales team. A CRM. A methodology the team got trained on two years ago. A coach who flies in once a quarter. A pipeline review every Friday that everyone half-attends.

And revenue still wobbles.

Quarter to quarter. The forecast is a guess dressed up as a number. Good months feel like luck. Bad months feel like a personal failing.

I’ve been in those rooms for twenty years. First as a founder building a £27M business. Next as a business coach that would fly in to facilitate quarterly planning sessions and now as the CEO of an organisation that’s implemented this work across five hundred companies.

The pattern is so consistent it stopped being interesting and started being useful.

**Here’s the thing nobody names.**

Every good business has a **business operating system**. It’s how they run meetings. Set priorities. Hold people accountable. The CEO can describe it in two sentences.

Ask the same CEO what their **sales operating system** is. You’ll get a list.

*“We have a CRM.”*

*“We have a sales playbook.”*

*“We just hired a head of sales.”*

*“We did training last year.”*

None of those are a sales operating system. They are inputs. Tools. Assets. They sit around the system that doesn’t exist yet.

> A CRM records what happened. It doesn’t create what happens next.

Sales training tells your team what to say. It doesn’t change what they do on Tuesday at 10am when they have ten minutes between meetings and they have to choose between making a proactive call or refreshing their inbox.

A coach can light a fire. They cannot implement a daily behaviour across 22 customer-facing people who will quietly opt out the moment the coach leaves the room and does not follow up till next quarter.

The category is missing.

**Here’s what it looks like when you implement Outgrow® Sales Operating System.**

I worked with a founder last quarter who told me his sales team makes 200 calls a week. Revenue was flat.

I asked him how many of those 200 calls were proactive. Calls his team initiated. Not responses. Not renewals. Not reactions to a problem.

He didn’t know. Nobody in his business did.

We added one number to the weekly scorecard. Proactive calls per person.

Four weeks later that number was 38. The team had been calling. They had just been **responding**, not **initiating**. The behaviour that drives growth was missing. Nobody could see it until we counted it.

I call this **behaviour blindness**. It is the gap between activity and revenue, hiding in plain sight on every dashboard that only counts what already happened.

A sales operating system closes that gap.

**What a sales operating system like Outgrow® actually does.**

It treats every customer-facing person as a revenue role. Service techs. Project managers. Account managers. Customer success. Not just the sales team.

It runs on behaviours, not pipeline. We track what people do, not what they hope. Behaviours are leading. Pipeline is lagging. By the time pipeline tells you something is wrong, it already is.

It builds in the support that humans actually need. Mindset coaching every week. Relaunches built into year two and year three because the work will fade. That is not a deficiency. That is the nature of asking humans to take extra actions. Every methodology that has pretended otherwise has watched its implementation die or wither after novelty wears off.

**The CEO objection.**

*“We already have a CRM. We already have a sales team. Why do I need another thing?”*

You don’t need another thing.

You need the thing all those things are sitting around but never quite become.

A CRM is a filing cabinet. A sales team is a group of people. A methodology is a slide deck. None of them, alone or together, install the daily behaviour that makes 15 to 30 percent of growth predictable.

**Outgrow® does.**

The category is missing. So I’m naming it.

**The question to sit with.**

If I asked your top ten customers what proactive contact looks like from your business in the last 90 days, what would they say?

Not the call when something went wrong. Not the renewal call. The contact where you reached out to ask what else you could help with. No agenda. Nothing was wrong when you called them.

Most CEOs already know the answer.

That answer is the size of the gap.

That gap is where the Outgrow® goes.

It’s not charisma or credentials — just 10 simple, repeatable habits that change everything.

## The 10 Habits That Separate Bad Bosses from Great Ones

I came across this visual today on LinkedIn and it nails the difference between bosses people *tolerate* and bosses people *follow*.

![Image from the essay “The 10 habits that separate bad bosses from great ones”](https://sidjashnani.com/assets/blog/the-10-habits-that-separate-bad-bosses/7c93168e4b44dd5d.webp)

It’s not personality.

It’s not degrees.

It’s habits.

Here’s the cheat sheet:

1. **Listens more than talks** → Great bosses absorb before they advise.
2. **Owns outcomes** → Doesn’t play the blame game. Ever.
3. **Shares context** → Gives people the *why*, not just the *what*.
4. **Has the hard talks** → Handles issues early, calmly, directly.
5. **Delegates decisions** → Trusts people to figure it out.
6. **Is consistent** → No mood swings, no surprises.
7. **Makes time** → Available, not just approachable.
8. **Reviews, not reacts** → Looks back quarterly, not emotionally.
9. **Says thank you** → Quickly, sincerely, specifically.
10. **Focuses forward** → Builds systems so the team can run without them.

The best bosses aren’t the loudest.

They’re the clearest.

They don’t need to “motivate” people.

Their habits *do* it for them.

---

**Bottom line:**

You don’t need to read another leadership or management book to become a great boss (though if you insist, *[How to Be a Great Boss](https://www.amazon.co.uk/How-Great-Boss-Gino-Wickman/dp/1942952848)*[by Gino Wickman](https://www.amazon.co.uk/How-Great-Boss-Gino-Wickman/dp/1942952848) won’t hurt your brain).

Just do these 10 things daily, consistently, almost obsessively.

Because leadership isn’t a chapter you read. It’s a muscle you train every day.

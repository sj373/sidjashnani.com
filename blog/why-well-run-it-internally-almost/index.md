# Why “we’ll run it internally” almost never gets run

- Date: 15 September 2026
- Mirror: https://sidjashnani.com/blog/why-well-run-it-internally-almost/
- Original: https://sidjashnani.substack.com/p/why-well-run-it-internally-almost
- Author: Sid Jashnani, CEO of Outgrow and Outgrow Certified Advisor
- Type: Letter

Two questions: why haven’t you done this before, and what will be different going forward?

Two hours into the session. The whiteboard is covered. The leadership team is nodding along, agreeing with every single point.

Then the owner leans back in his chair:

“This is great. But honestly, I think we can just run this ourselves.”

Nobody in that room is lying.

They are smart, capable people. They have a CRM. They have weekly leadership meetings. They have a competent sales manager. On paper, there is zero reason they could not build and install the entire operating rhythm tomorrow morning.

Yet they almost never do.

> **Over the years, I learned never to argue when an owner says that. Arguing turns the room into a contest, and you never win a contest with a founder in their own boardroom.**

Instead, I ask two simple questions.

> **First: “Why haven’t you done this already?”**

Then I stop talking.

The honest answer usually surfaces after a short pause. We have been busy firefighting. We talked about doing it last Q4. We kicked off an initiative two years ago, but daily client demands took over and it drifted.

Then comes the second question.

> **“What is actually going to be different this time?”**

That is the uncomfortable one.

Because the person assigned to enforce the daily outreach, hold people accountable to the call log, and inspect the inputs is the exact same person who has to sit next to them at lunch tomorrow.

When accountability is internal, grading gets soft.

A team member misses their daily calls for three days straight because an operational fire broke out. The manager lets it slide because they understand the stress. By week three, proactive calls become optional again. By month two, the entire system has quietly evaporated into the background.

I once watched a single division inside a mid-market distributor attempt to run the playbook entirely on their own. That one division managed about 20% growth because the department head was unusually stubborn.

The rest of the business? Completely flat.

Same company. Same materials. Totally different execution.

Nobody grades their own homework honestly. Not because people are malicious or lazy, but because we naturally protect workplace harmony over awkward compliance conversations.

> **Artificial harmony only causes problems.**

The issue was never capability.

“We can do this ourselves” is rarely a strategy.

More often, it is an unconscious admission that everyone likes the idea of the result, but nobody inside the building wants the daily friction of enforcing the standard.

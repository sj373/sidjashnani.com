A proven way to grow revenue without founder heroics

A founder said something to me recently that I cannot shake.

**“Take me out of the equation, and sales will collapse.”**

He was not proud of it. He was exhausted by it.

And I have heard versions of this sentence for seven years as a business coach. Different words. Same problem.

These were not broken companies.

Clear vision. Good people in the right seats. Strong accountability. Solid processes.

And yet sales still felt fragile. Personal. Founder dependent.

That question kept bothering me.

Why do some companies grow predictably, while others struggle every quarter?

---

## The pattern I kept seeing

The struggling companies were not lazy or incompetent.

They simply did not have a **system for sales**. They were banking on founder-led sales and their relationships or were waiting for their phone to ring.

They had systems for operations. Systems for finance. Systems for hiring.

But revenue was reactive.

Waiting for inbound leads. Hoping the pipeline fills. Leaning on the founder’s relationships to close deals.

Hope is not a strategy. I learned that painfully in my first two businesses.

The companies that grew consistently did one thing differently.

They took swings (actions) every single day. Not when things were desperate. Not at quarter end.

Daily. Relentless. Boring. Effective.

## What makes Outgrow different

**1. Sales is a habit, not a department.** Most companies outsource growth to the sales team or the founder. Outgrow involves every customer facing role. Everyone who touches a customer can create opportunity (or kill it).

**2. It is about doing, not training** I have sat through enough sales workshops that spike energy for a week and disappear. Outgrow is daily action. Choreographed work. Consistent swings.

**3. It creates positivity, not pressure** No fear tactics. No manipulation. Just habits that feel good and produce results. People want to do the work because they see it working.

More swings (actions) equals more hits (revenue).

> Control the controllables. Let the results follow.

That is the missing piece I was looking for.

# No Activity, No Revenue

- Date: 21 September 2026
- Mirror: https://sidjashnani.com/blog/no-activity-no-revenue/
- Original: https://sidjashnani.substack.com/p/no-activity-no-revenue
- Author: Sid Jashnani, CEO of Outgrow and Outgrow Certified Advisor
- Type: Letter

When the diary falls apart, the very first thing founders drop is the only thing that actually feeds the pipeline.

My luggage was stuck somewhere in transit, the calendar for the rest of the week was completely derailed, and an in-person revenue growth workshop was still waiting for me to be delivered.

![Image from the essay “No Activity, No Revenue”](https://sidjashnani.com/assets/blog/no-activity-no-revenue/ffd5e190d16adca6.webp)

My immediate instinct, which is probably the same instinct most of us have when things go wrong, was to start complaining. I wanted to blame the airport management, the weather, and the ground crew, giving myself full permission to write off the entire afternoon. But as I opened my notepad to list the things I needed to get done, I glanced at a short reminder I had posted down few weeks ago during another messy week.

**It simply said that complaining about a lack of results is usually just an excuse to hide from the activity that creates them.**

That note stung, mostly because it brought back a very specific, very uncomfortable memory from when I had first moved across the Arabian sea to live in Dubai.

![Image from the essay “No Activity, No Revenue”](https://sidjashnani.com/assets/blog/no-activity-no-revenue/e62cfbfdf63d4e2d.webp)

I had convinced myself that I had reached a stage in my career where the standard rules of commercial legwork no longer applied to me. I started suffering from what I now call Elite Surgeon Syndrome. I thought of myself like a top specialist on Harley Street, someone who sits quietly in a private consulting room while clients queue up down the hall, wait their turn, and politely ask for an appointment.

Because I believed I was above the basic, unglamorous work of picking up the phone and following up, I completely stopped doing it. I told myself that outbound follow-up was undignified, that my reputation would carry the load, and that serious people wait for inbound demand.

Meanwhile, living costs and the monthly business burn rate remained entirely real. Dubai is an expensive place to entertain delusions. The hunger slowly evaporated, but the overheads did not. It took an uncomfortably long time, and a painful amount of burned cash, to admit the truth to myself. I was not operating like an elite specialist. I was just hiding behind my ego because I thought I had outgrown the basics.

**Whenever revenue stalls in a business, founders almost always look for complicated explanations.**

They tell themselves they have a positioning issue, or an offer structure problem, or that the market needs a completely new strategic narrative. What is actually happening is much simpler and much harder to face. When the diary blows up, or when a good year whispers in your ear that you have arrived, the very first thing you drop is the daily commercial legwork.

That drift is human, and I know how easily I slip into it myself. It is genuinely the main reason Outgrow exists in my working life today. Alex Goldfayn created the system, and when I stepped in as CEO of Outgrow LLC, it was not because I thought I had solved sales forever. It was because I needed an operating system that would protect me from my own complacency.

![Image from the essay “No Activity, No Revenue”](https://sidjashnani.com/assets/blog/no-activity-no-revenue/5bcd5cedd483a944.webp)

At its heart, the Outgrow methodology is a disciplined, weekly rhythm of proactive outreach to people you already know. You reach out to existing clients and past relationships before they ask. You look for ways to be genuinely useful to them, you track your swings honestly, ask for referrals, and you stay humble enough to pick up the phone week after week. Not through clever tricks, but through pure consistency.

Do not spend Sunday tweaking your deck or rewriting your website copy.

**Shrink your horizon right down to today. The work will still feel unglamorous, but your pipeline will start moving again.**

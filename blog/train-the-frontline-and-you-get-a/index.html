Train the frontline and you get a good week. Train the manager and you get a good year.

Sid Jashnani, CEO of Outgrow and Outgrow Certified Advisor

Stop installing your revenue system in the wrong place.

Originally published on Substack (opens in a new tab). Subscribe (opens in a new tab) for the next letter.

Most founders install a new sales habit at the frontline.

They gather the sellers. They teach the behaviour. Everyone nods. It works for a fortnight. Then it fades.

Not because the people are weak. Because the habit had nobody to hold it.

Alex Goldfayn put a number on this that I keep coming back to. Working with managers is roughly 80/20 more impactful than working with the frontline. Same effort, four times the return, because a manager doesn’t do the behaviour once. A manager multiplies it across a team, every week, for as long as they run the team.

Teach a seller and you’ve changed one person’s Tuesday. Teach a manager to run the system and you’ve changed how forty people work for a year.

What the manager actually holds

The behaviour survives because someone runs a weekly huddle around it. Not a status meeting. A short, repeatable rhythm with four parts.

Review the scorecard. Who did the proactive work last week, who didn’t, what did it produce.

Share the wins. Read out a real one. A dormant customer reopened, a quote chased down, an order size that grew. Wins are contagious, and they teach faster than instruction.

Prescribe the customers. Name who each person calls this week. Not “make some calls.” These customers, by name.

Prescribe the products. Point at what to talk about, so the calls have a purpose beyond checking in.

Fifteen minutes. Every week. Same shape.

The one rule that holds it together

Always connect the actions to revenue. Never track actions in isolation.

The moment a huddle becomes “did you make your calls,” it dies, because it feels like surveillance. When it stays “here’s the revenue those calls opened,” it lives, because everyone can see the point.

That’s the difference between a manager policing activity and a manager leading growth.

Why founders get this wrong

Because the frontline is where the selling looks like it happens. So that’s where the attention goes.

But the frontline is where the behaviour is done. The manager’s chair is where the behaviour is kept alive. Install it there and it compounds. Install it below there and you’re re-teaching it every month until you give up and call the system a failure.

It wasn’t the system. It was the level you installed it at.

Outgrow is built to live in the manager’s weekly rhythm. Sales doing, not sales training. The frontline makes the calls. The manager makes sure next week they still do.

The close

A seller can give you a good week.

A manager who runs the rhythm gives you a good year.

Who in your business is actually holding the weekly rhythm your revenue depends on, and what happens to it the week they stop?

# The one question that turns "really keen" into money.

They said yes. So why isn't it a sale?

**“Yes, this makes complete sense.”**

You’ve heard it. It feels like the finish line.

It isn’t. A yes without a date is just a nice feeling you both had in a room. And feelings cool.

So after the yes, I ask one question.

“When would you like to launch?”

If they give me a date, wonderful. It goes in the calendar and the deal has somewhere to live.

If they don’t, I don’t retreat. I climb down the ladder.

No date? “Which month are you picturing?” No month? “This quarter or next? Before the summer or after?” No quarter? “Is this a this-year decision, or a next-year one?”

Each rung asks for less. Each rung still asks for something.

### Why this works

The power is not the information. It’s the commitment the question raises.

A prospect who says “September” has done something the prospect who says “soon” hasn’t. They’ve pictured it. They’ve put it on a mental calendar. They’ve made it real to themselves, in their own words, out loud.

And here is what I’ve watched happen more than once. A client starts growing revenue the moment they decide to run Outgrow. Before the launch. Before the first prescribed call. The decision itself changes how the team shows up on Monday, because now there is a date, and a date organises behaviour the way a wish never does.

### The prospect who genuinely can’t start yet

Sometimes the timing is real. They can’t begin for months.

Fine. Offer to hold the slot. A small deposit secures the date.

Framed right, that’s a gift to them, not a hoop for you. They plan around it. They’ve committed to their own calendar, not just to a warm chat with you.

### The mistake I made for years

I treated the yes as the close.

So I’d send a friendly “just checking in,” get a friendly “still keen,” and six weeks later the warmth had faded to nothing. Nobody said no. The deal just never had a date to stand on.

Interest decays. A date does not.

Outgrow runs on commitments, logged and reviewed, not on good feelings remembered.

### The close

A yes tells you they like it. A date tells you they’ll do it.

Which deal in your pipeline is “really keen” with no date on it? That’s not a win yet. That’s your next call.

## THE COMMITMENT LADDER

After the yes, get a date. If you can’t get a date, get a rung. Ask the top rung first, then climb down one step at a time until they commit to something.

**1. Date** — “When would you like to launch?” → a calendar entry; the deal has somewhere to live. **2. Month** — “Which month are you picturing?” → they’ve placed it in real time. **3. Quarter** — “This quarter or next? Before summer or after?” → a window you can plan and follow up against. **4. Year** — “Is this a this-year decision, or a next-year one?” → the truth about how real it is.

**The rule:** the power isn’t the information you gather. It’s the commitment the question raises. Saying “September” out loud does something “soon” never will.

**For the prospect who genuinely can’t start for months, the deposit move:**

> “Sounds like the timing is a few months out. Want me to hold the slot for you? A small deposit secures the date so you can plan around it.”

Framed as their benefit, not your hoop. They commit to their own calendar.

A yes tells you they like it. A date tells you they’ll do it.

Interest decays; a date does not.

# Your team isn’t too busy to call. They’re too scared.

- Date: 25 April 2026
- Mirror: https://sidjashnani.com/blog/your-team-isnt-too-busy-to-call-theyre/
- Original: https://sidjashnani.substack.com/p/your-team-isnt-too-busy-to-call-theyre
- Author: Sid Jashnani, CEO of Outgrow and Outgrow Certified Advisor
- Type: Letter

Three calls. Five minutes. The habit your team won't do, and the lie they tell themselves about why

Every week, a founder tells me the same thing.

> “My team is too busy to call customers.”

Sometimes it’s “we don’t want to pester them.” Sometimes it’s “they’ll call us if they need something.” Sometimes it’s dressed up in the language of a busy operator: “we’re prioritising delivery.”

It’s the same answer every time. And it’s never the real reason.

The real reason your team isn’t picking up the phone has nothing to do with time. It’s fear. Until you accept that, no amount of CRM, training, or sales kickoff is going to move your revenue.

## The maths is offensive

Let me show you what “too busy” actually looks like.

Three proactive calls a day. Per person. That is the entire ask.

A proactive call, in the Outgrow system, is a call to a customer or prospect when nothing is wrong. No problem to fix. No urgent matter. You ring someone you have a relationship with and ask what they have coming up that you can help with. That’s it.

Three of those a day. Most go to voicemail. Most reps spend less than five minutes a day doing this work.

Five minutes. Per day.

If you have ten customer-facing people doing this, that’s 7,500 proactive conversations a year your business wasn’t having before. Two-thirds of those voicemails get a return communication when followed up with a text. That’s the Outgrow VM+Text technique, and the response rate holds up across 500+ companies and 100 million data points.

Five thousand return conversations from five minutes a day per person.

Tell me again how your team is too busy.

## The real objection

When a founder tells me the team doesn’t have time, I ask one question. “When was the last time you made one of these calls yourself?”

The answer is almost always the same. A long pause. Then some version of “it’s been a while.”

That’s the actual problem.

The team isn’t avoiding the calls because they’re busy. They’re avoiding them because nobody at the top is doing them. When the founder doesn’t make proactive calls, the team learns that proactive calls aren’t real work. The behaviour you tolerate is the behaviour you teach.

The other version of this objection, “we don’t want to pester our customers,” is just fear with better marketing.

Nobody is pestered by a five-minute call from someone they trust who asks if there’s anything they can help with. That isn’t pestering. That’s caring. Your customer doesn’t see your silence as respect. They see it as indifference. And when a competitor finally rings them, they answer.

## What the silence is actually costing you

Take your top twenty accounts. Ask yourself, honestly, what percentage of their relevant spend you currently hold. Not what you’d guess. What you actually know.

Most founders, when they push their team to find this number, get a brutal answer. Sometimes 5%. Sometimes less.

The other 95% is going to a competitor who is making the calls your team isn’t.

Three proactive calls a day per person, sustained for a year, is how that gap closes. Not a campaign. Not a quarterly push. A daily habit.

The companies adding 15 to 30% in revenue every year aren’t doing anything radical. They aren’t running clever campaigns. They’ve built one habit into their team’s day and protected it like sacred ground.

Five minutes. Three calls. Every day.

The maths works. The system works. The only question left is whether you’re willing to make the first call yourself, today, before you ask anyone else to.

That’s where it always starts.

## One thing to do this week

Pick three customers. Not prospects. Customers you already work with. People who would take your call.

Ring them. Leave a voicemail if they don’t pick up. Follow up with a text. Ask what they have coming up that you can help with.

If you can’t find five minutes for that, the problem isn’t your sales team.
